## Observations after 100 records

**The carbon column is effectively empty.** Four records in a hundred, and on inspection none evaluates a data architecture on carbon: #12 measures pipeline energy inside a mesh, #36 is a cloud life-cycle impact method, #38 is a green-cloud SLR, #67 mentions energy among fog-cloud trade-offs. The claim for Chapter 2 is therefore narrower and stronger than "carbon is rarely discussed": carbon appears in the corpus only as a property of cloud infrastructure or of individual pipelines, never as a dimension on which architectures are compared.

**The weight-source column is the gap claim in numbers.** Of 100 records, 33 perform weighting of some kind. Of those, one reports a panel with a stated size (three departmental directors of a single buying organisation), four mention a panel without stating its composition, eleven openly assign weights by author judgment, and twelve cannot be determined from the abstract. No record elicits weights from practitioners across organisations, and none reports an agreement measure.

**The second batch is domain-heavy, and the shift is informative.** Records 51–100 contain far more mesh, lakehouse and fabric work and far fewer cloud-selection studies. Method use collapses accordingly: 34 of these 50 records apply no decision method at all. The two halves of the corpus behave differently — the domain literature describes architectures without deciding between them, the methods literature decides between cloud services without addressing architectures. That separation is the two-layer gap made visible at record level, and it is worth stating exactly this way in §2.7.

**Governance dominates the domain side.** SECC rises from 44% to 66% in the second batch, driven by mesh and fabric papers where federated governance is the central concern. This supports retaining SECC in the criteria set, and simultaneously raises the discriminance question: if nearly every domain paper addresses governance, the criterion may separate configurations weakly. Flag for the discriminance check after matrix coding.
