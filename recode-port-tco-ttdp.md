## What changed and why

**TCO falls by a third.** Two patterns account for almost all of it. First, records claiming their own artefact is cost-efficient — "cloud-native solutions that offer greater cost efficiency", "low-cost storage environments" — were coded `yes` in v1. Under the v2 test a claimed benefit is not an evaluation dimension, so these become `no`. Second, and more embarrassing, **several v1 codings were triggered by the copyright line**: "© The Author(s), under exclusive license to Springer Nature" contains the word *license*, which the cost pattern matched. Records 3, 17, 48, 55, 56, 79, 80, 83, 91, 95 and 115 were affected. This is the same class of error as the IFIP "federation" false positive in OpModel, and it is a strong argument for coding from sentences rather than term proximity.

**PORT halves.** The v1 coding treated any occurrence of "interoperability" as portability. Under v2 the two senses are separated: *can I move or use another engine* counts, *can these systems exchange data* does not. Records concerned with semantic interoperability between organisations, clinical systems or policy domains — 2, 6, 11, 30, 48, 50, 52, 55, 62, 78, 89, 91, 101, 139 — become `no`. What survives is a coherent and much more meaningful set: open table formats, vendor lock-in, multi-engine access, migration cost, multi-cloud.

**TTDP falls to six.** In v1 it was applied to query latency, streaming performance and processing throughput. Under the explicit rule that system performance is not delivery time, only six records remain: 46 (production ML capabilities deployed months ahead), 96 (shortening the software development cycle), 104 and 105 (implementation timeframe and ease of adoption among the criteria), 128 (installation speed weighed against ease of use), and 11 (time from detection to action, which is arguably an outcome metric rather than a delivery metric — flagged as borderline).

## Implications

**The criteria coverage map changes shape, and the story it tells sharpens.**

| Criterion | Records | Share |
|---|---|---|
| SECC | 76 | 53% |
| TCO | 52 | 36% |
| AIRD | 31 | 22% |
| PORT | 26 | 18% |
| CARB | 8 | 6% |
| **TTDP** | **6** | **4%** |

Under v1 the map showed a gentle gradient from SECC at 53% down to CARB at 6%. Under v2 the distribution is sharply bimodal: governance and cost are addressed by a third to a half of the corpus, while **delivery speed is addressed by four percent of it and carbon by six**. TTDP is now the second-rarest criterion, not the fourth.

**This is a stronger finding than the one it replaces.** Delivery time is the criterion practitioners talk about constantly — it is the whole premise of the DORA metrics in software engineering — and it appears as an evaluation dimension in six of 143 studies of data architecture selection. Two of those six are the Espinoza pair, and they operationalise it as *ease of adoption* rather than elapsed time. **No study in the corpus measures how long it takes to deliver a data product.** That is a cleaner gap statement than anything available under v1, and it directly supports the TTDP metric passport's insistence on an operational endpoint definition.

**A note for the protocol.** Both false-positive families found in this round — "federation" in a publisher name, "license" in a copyright line — arose from matching terms rather than reading sentences. The extraction procedure description in §2.2.6 should state that coding was performed by reading abstracts in full, and that the validation round surfaced and corrected this class of error. An examiner who notices the copyright-line problem independently will be far less troubled if the thesis has already named it.
