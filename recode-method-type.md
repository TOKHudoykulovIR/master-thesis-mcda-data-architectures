**What changed and why.** The `fuzzy-variant` column now exists as a co-code rather than as a substitute. In v1 a paper applying fuzzy TOPSIS was recorded sometimes as `TOPSIS`, sometimes as `fuzzy-variant` — the inconsistency that drove Method's κ to 0.44. Twelve records now carry both labels, which keeps the base-method tally comparable while preserving the information that the method was applied under uncertainty.

`other` rises because several v1 codings named a technique the abstract did not: record 9 was coded `TOPSIS+Markov` where the abstract describes only a three-phase MCDM framework; record 13 was coded `fuzzy+entropy` where entropy governs weighting, not ranking. Both are now `other`.

Two records lose a method entirely. Record 68 compares three clinical data architectures descriptively — no decision procedure is applied, so `none`, not `other`. Record 132 proposes an assessment model without a ranking mechanism.

**For the methods inventory in §2.5.** Of 143 records, **57 apply a decision method** and 72 apply none. Within the 57: TOPSIS and its variants dominate at 11 records, AHP and ANP together 10, BWM 3, PROMETHEE 1, ELECTRE 1, plain weighted scoring 4, and 34 use bespoke or non-standard procedures. Twelve apply their method under fuzzy, neutrosophic or intuitionistic uncertainty.

**Three observations worth a sentence each in the text.** First, BWM appears in only 3 of 143 records, all in cloud-service selection — its application to data architectures is unattested in this corpus. Second, no record combines BWM with an outranking method, which is the pipeline this thesis uses. Third, the prevalence of `other` (34 records, 60% of those applying a method) indicates a field that reaches for bespoke procedures rather than established MCDA techniques — relevant when justifying the choice of conventional, well-documented operators in Chapter 3.

## Type — corpus totals

| Value | v1 | **v2** |

**What changed and why.** The ordering rule resolved the two ambiguities that drove κ to 0.57.

*Framework before case study.* Records 78, 118 and 132 propose a transferable approach and demonstrate it on one organisation; v1 coded them `case study` because a single organisation appears. Rule 4 now precedes rule 5, so they are `framework`. Records that genuinely report one organisation's experience without proposing a general artefact — 16, 46, 51, 66, 79, 96, 102, 139 — remain `case study`.

*Empirical before framework.* Records 12 and 134 report measurements; rule 1 catches them first. Record 1 compares three table formats through measurement rather than proposing anything, so `empirical` rather than `framework`.

*Review requires the survey to be the contribution.* Record 91 reviews FAIR principles in order to analyse their fit with data mesh — the analysis is the contribution, so `conceptual`. Record 56 combines a literature review with expert interviews to compare two paradigms; the comparison is the contribution, so `conceptual` rather than `framework`.

**Observation for §2.3.** The corpus splits almost evenly between records proposing a decision procedure (56) and records proposing or describing an architecture (45 framework + 22 conceptual = 67). The two halves barely overlap — a point already visible in the Arch and Method fields, now corroborated by the contribution type itself.
