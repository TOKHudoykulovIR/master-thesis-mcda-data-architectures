## Findings across the full corpus

**Carbon.** Eight records in 143. On inspection none evaluates a data architecture on carbon: #12 measures pipeline energy inside a mesh; #36 and #38 address cloud infrastructure generally; #67 notes energy among fog-cloud trade-offs; #119 selects a storage technology *for* LCA work rather than assessing the storage technology's own footprint; #127 selects a warehouse schema on energy; #130 studies query-processor energy on multicore hardware; #134 models data-lake energy with a CTMC. Three of the eight (#127, #130, #134) do measure energy in a data-architecture context, and all three operate at the level of a component — a schema, a query processor, a management strategy — with absolute consumption as the quantity. **No record normalises emissions to a functional unit of delivered work, and none uses the resulting measure as a criterion for choosing between architectures.**

**Weight elicitation.** Of 143 records, 57 perform weighting of some kind. Within those: 22 assign weights by author judgment; 22 do not state the source in the abstract; 8 mention a panel without stating its size or composition; 3 derive weights objectively by entropy; 5 draw on consumer, user or provider input; 3 combine literature with interviews. **Exactly one record reports a panel with a stated size** — three departmental directors of a single buying organisation. **No record elicits weights from practitioners across multiple organisations, and no record reports an inter-rater agreement measure.**

**The two-layer structure at record level.** The corpus separates cleanly. Records addressing cloud services or providers — the SS3 methodological group — apply decision methods routinely but never address data architectures. Records addressing data architectures — mesh, lakehouse, fabric, warehouse — describe, compare and prescribe, but 73 of 143 apply no decision method at all. The methods literature decides without addressing architectures; the domain literature addresses architectures without deciding. The intersection, populated by the Espinoza pair (#104, #105), Zhou & Chen (#14), Srivastava (#32) and Tlemcani et al. (#81), contains five records, none of which combines empirical elicitation with a sustainability criterion.

**Operating model.** Coded FED in 29 records, all of them mesh or federated-architecture papers; CENT in 4; HUB in 1 (#33, which distinguishes logically from physically decentralised meshes and is the only record approaching a middle position). In every case the operating model is a property of the paradigm under discussion, never a dimension varied against a platform choice. **No record in the corpus treats platform and operating model as independent axes.**

**Discriminance warning.** SECC and TCO both appear in 53% of records, but their distribution differs sharply: SECC concentrates in the domain group, TCO in the methods group. If the domain literature addresses governance almost universally, SECC may separate configurations weakly during matrix coding. Retained as a flag for the discriminance check.

## Coding notes

*Espinoza weights.* Records #104 and #105 are coded `panel (internal team, n unstated)` on the basis of full-text extraction, which shows independent scoring by a cross-functional team within one organisation with no participant count reported. The abstract alone would not support this coding; noted so the reduced and full extractions remain reconcilable.

*The `unclear` count is reportable.* Twenty-two abstracts state that an MCDM method was applied without naming the weight source. These are not silently folded into author judgment. Chapter 2 should phrase the claim over the 35 records where the source is determinable, and report the 22 separately.

*Borderline carbon codings.* #119 is marked CARB because storage-technology selection is framed around life-cycle assessment data, though the assessed footprint is the product's rather than the platform's. Flagged rather than excluded, since the distinction matters for the precision of the Chapter 2 claim.
